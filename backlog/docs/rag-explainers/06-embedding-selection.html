Phase G · task-31.6 · Embeddings

Embedding model selection: read the Retrieval column, not the headline

An embedding model is the lens through which every document and query enters your vector store. Pick it by its reputation and you optimise a number that has nothing to do with retrieval. Pick it by measurement — on your data, by the right metric — and the whole pipeline gets sharper for free.

01 The problem

Bergamot embeds every re-downloaded public page with OpenAI text-embedding-3-small by default. That model was chosen by reputation — it is popular, cheap, and "good enough" by hearsay — and its retrieval quality on Bergamot's own corpus has never been measured. We are trusting a default we cannot defend with a number.

An embedding maps a piece of text to a vector: a fixed-length list of numbers (a few hundred to a few thousand) positioned so that texts with similar meaning land close together. Retrieval works by embedding the query and returning the chunks whose vectors sit nearest. So the embedding model is the retriever — everything downstream (reranking, generation) only re-orders what the embedding first found.

Failure mode

Teams pick the model topping the headline leaderboard average. But the best model overall is often not the best at retrieval — the one task RAG actually needs. Worse, the headline number says nothing about dimensions and cost: a model can win the average while producing larger, pricier vectors that inflate storage and query latency for no retrieval gain. Optimising the wrong column quietly degrades the whole pipeline.

02 How it works

The MTEB (Massive Text Embedding Benchmark) is the standard yardstick for embedding models. Its central idea is that "embedding quality" is not one number — it scores each model across eight task categories, because a vector that is excellent for one job can be mediocre for another.

MTEB's eight task categories. RAG cares about exactly one of them.

The MTEB paper's key finding: no single model dominates all eight categories. A model that tops the overall average may be carried there by Classification and Clustering while trailing on Retrieval. So for RAG you must read the Retrieval column, not the overall average.

Worked example: the average lies

From Modal's leaderboard writeup (numbers illustrative and time-sensitive): a model can lead the overall average yet trail on Retrieval. NV-Embed-v2 posts roughly 72.31 overall but only ~62.65 on Retrieval — behind Gemini Embedding 001 at ~67.71 Retrieval, even though Gemini sits lower on the headline average. If you chose by the average, you would pick the worse retriever.

Dimensions, cost, and Matryoshka

Each model also emits vectors of a fixed dimension (e.g. 768, 1536). Higher dimensions cost more: every vector takes more storage, and every similarity search does more arithmetic, so compute and latency rise too. A 1536-dim model costs roughly twice the storage of a 768-dim one for the same corpus.

Matryoshka embeddings (named after nested Russian dolls) are trained so the most important information is packed into the front of the vector. That lets you truncate a vector to fewer dimensions and keep most of its quality — a graceful loss rather than a cliff. You trade a little retrieval accuracy for a big storage saving, on a dial you control.

The right move

Don't trust headline averages. Benchmark your candidate models on your own golden dataset (measuring Recall and nDCG) and on cost, using the evaluation harness. The leaderboard narrows the field; your data picks the winner. All specific scores on this page are illustrative and time-sensitive — current leaderboards will differ.

04 Decision lens

When to reach for embedding selection — and when to skip it

✓ Reach for it when

  • You have a golden set to measure on (task-31.1) — without it you can't adjudicate.
  • Embedding cost or storage is material at your corpus scale.
  • You suspect a better-fit model exists for your domain and want proof.

✕ Skip / overkill when

  • Your incumbent already measures well on your data — don't churn models for leaderboard vanity.
  • You have no eval to adjudicate — build the harness first, then choose.
The tradeoff

Switching models forces a full re-embed of the corpus (compute + time), and possibly a dimension change in the vector schema. Only do it when the measured retrieval gain — or the cost saving — justifies the re-embed. Always pick by the Retrieval column on your own data, never the headline average.

05 In Bergamot

task-31.6 replaces the unmeasured default with a measured choice. It ships:

  • At least three embedding models benchmarked on the golden dataset by Retrieval metrics (Recall, nDCG) and cost.
  • The model chosen by measured retrieval quality — not overall MTEB average — with the rationale recorded.
  • The embedding model made swappable via config, so future re-benchmarks are a one-line change.
  • Matryoshka / dimension tradeoffs evaluated for storage savings against quality loss.
  • The corpus re-embedded under the winner, so live retrieval reflects the measured choice.
