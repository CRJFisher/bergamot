task-39 · privacy-core · umbrella

Privacy-core capture model

Privacy is bergamot's core organizing principle. Capture stores browsing metadata only — never page content: visit id, URL, page-load timestamp, title, and the navigation/session graph. That metadata is the durable source of truth. Page content is obtained later by re-downloading the public URL through a stealth headless browser running as an anonymous, cookie-free visitor — the login wall is the privacy filter, so authenticated and paywalled pages fail to render content and are excluded. Any cached content is encrypted, on-demand, scoped, and deletable. This task is the code and schema work that makes the product behave that way — the original capture/store/forget pillars, then the hardening that seals every remaining plaintext path on disk.

the why

Why metadata-first changes everything

Storing page content at browse time means the most private thing a user does — what they actually read — lives on disk as the source of truth, and demands a "is this sensitive?" judgement that is impossible to get right. The privacy-core model removes that liability at the root: the trail is kept, the contents are not. To understand a page, bergamot re-downloads it from its public URL as an anonymous stranger. A page that requires a login simply will not come back — so the private ones exclude themselves, with no heuristic to trust. The same principle then drives the hardening: every byte of metadata that lands on disk must be encrypted, bounded, and deletable by the forget cascade.

admissibility — the spine, not a feature

Per the intention tree, this advances the CAPTURE and STORE nodes directly: capture becomes metadata-only and deterministic; the metadata store becomes the durable, encryptable source of truth. Every UNDERSTAND/SURFACE consumer (TDT, then RAG) reads only what re-download yields.

task-39.7keyringto do

Surface Linux keyring degradation of store keys

The store-encryption key is held in the OS keyring via SecretStorage. On Linux with no working keyring, Electron safeStorage silently swaps in a hardcoded wrapping key — SecretStorage still works, but the at-rest defense drops to obfuscation. A one-time D-Bus probe detects this and shows a dismissible warning.

downstream contract

The corpus is the re-downloadable public subset

The keystone fetcher exposes a ContentCorpus contract (get_content, iter_public_pages). TDT (task-36) clusters this corpus first; RAG (task-31) retrieves over it after. Visits whose pages fail to re-download — auth-walled, dead, paywalled, non-HTML — stay as trail/metadata only and are excluded from clustering and retrieval. That coverage boundary is deliberate, and it is set entirely by the keystone fetcher. The task-31 and task-36 rescopes are tracked under their own parents.
